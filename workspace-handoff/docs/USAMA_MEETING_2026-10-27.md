## Eight questions to prioritize

1. **What role do you want me to play?** Which sites/products, what outcomes in the first month, and who currently handles development, hosting and support?
2. **What is already working commercially?** Which professions buy, which templates they choose, orders actually paid/fulfilled in the last 90 days, repeat/renewal business, and the main acquisition channel. Ask for aggregate evidence; customer identities are unnecessary.
3. **What is the actual workload?** Expected weekly hours, evening availability, remote/on-site days, urgent incidents, support responsibilities, response expectations, and whether design/content/data entry are included.
4. **What cash budget and payment terms are available?** Salary versus contractor retainer/project fee, fixed payment date, trial payment, infrastructure/tool budget, and who approves additional work.
5. **Can EdgeTap continue?** Disclose its overlap before agreeing to work. Discuss existing brand/code/customers, future independent development, conflicts, confidentiality, client solicitation and any exclusivity in writing.
6. **What technology and rights exist?** Custom or licensed platform; source/repository control; license terms and modification/update rights; domains/hosting/backups; current maintainer; and who owns new deliverables. Ask permission before any private technical audit.
7. **If equity is discussed, what exactly is it?** Legal entity or specific product, current owners, number/class of shares or options, fully diluted percentage, vesting, voting/information rights, dilution and departure terms.
8. **What is the smallest paid pilot?** One agreed measurable improvement with a capped scope and acceptance test, followed by a review of working together.

Business-learning follow-ups if time allows: print cost per acceptable card including rejects, supplier vs owned-machine economics, setup time per order, reprint handling, delivery density, renewal workload, and why customers choose a paid plan. These help assess the operating model; equipment and a large template gallery alone do not establish profitable demand.

## Salary, retainer, equity and royalty

| Arrangement | What it pays for | What to establish |
|---|---|---|
| Salary | An employment role and its agreed duties | Hours, responsibilities, cash pay/date, benefits, leave and outside-work terms |
| Contractor retainer | A defined amount of ongoing service capacity | Included hours/tasks, overage, response windows, expenses and termination notice |
| Project fee | A bounded deliverable | Acceptance, milestones/payment, revision limit, IP terms and ongoing support priced separately |
| Equity | Ownership or a contractual path to ownership | Actual instrument/entity, percentage basis, rights, vesting and possible dilution/liquidity |
| Revenue share | A contractual payment tied to defined sales/receipts | Eligible products, calculation/deductions, term, reporting and payment rights |
| Licence royalty | Payment for using IP under a licence | Who owns the IP, permitted use, royalty unit/base, term, minimums and exit/continuing rights |

Cash pay and equity can be combined. Startup equity may be illiquid and should be considered alongside the salary being given up; it is not spendable monthly income. [Carta on salary and equity](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/employee-equity/).

A licence royalty usually compensates the right to use IP and can depend on sales or use. Developing/maintaining a client's site does not by itself create an automatic royalty entitlement. If he owns the commissioned deliverable, a negotiated fee/retainer and possible revenue share may describe the arrangement more accurately. IP Australia explains the general [licence-royalty model](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/manage-my-ip/how-to-commercialise-my-ip/how-to-license-my-ip/licence-royalties); that is educational guidance, not Pakistan contract or tax advice.

## Is 30% or 40% equity good?

Either could be meaningful in a viable business with suitable rights, or offer little practical value if the entity/assets/terms are unclear. Do not treat 40% as automatically better than a smaller stake plus reliable cash, or treat the percentage as a valuation. Distinguish joining an existing profitable company from becoming a technical cofounder in a new venture; the required contribution, risk and authority differ.

Before comparing an equity offer, establish:

- **What owns the value:** which company holds the product, source/IP or licences, domains, customer contracts and relevant equipment; which revenues/costs belong to it; and any existing debt or ownership commitments.
- **What you receive:** shares, options, partnership interest or only a profit-share promise; exact quantity/class, price or exercise cost if any; and percentage on a fully diluted basis including existing promises/options.
- **How it is earned:** start date, time/milestone vesting, cliff, credit for prior work, who can terminate and what happens to earned/unearned rights. What is already earned by each founder should be transparent.
- **What you can decide and inspect:** technical/product authority, budget approval, voting/reserved matters and regular financial information. A large minority economic stake does not by itself settle management authority.
- **How cash might reach you:** salary separately, distribution policy, sale/exit arrangements and any priority rights ahead of ordinary shareholders. Ownership is not the same as receiving that percentage of monthly revenue.
- **What happens later:** dilution, new funding, share transfers, departure/buyback pricing, disputes and restrictions on EdgeTap.

Vesting means earning the grant over time or milestones. A cliff delays initial earning; YC describes four years with a one-year cliff as a common Silicon Valley founder example, not a mandatory or automatically suitable local term. Discuss the risk of losing unearned equity if work ends before the cliff. [YC on founder splits and vesting](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/splitting-equity-among-founders/).

Dilution means later share issuance can reduce the percentage: in a simplified example, a 30% holding becomes 24% if new investors receive 20% of the company after issuance and all existing holders dilute proportionally. [Carta on dilution](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/share-dilution/).

For a Pakistan company, verify the legal instrument and company records with suitable local advice. SECP distinguishes a shareholder from a member recorded in the company's register; a casual percentage statement is not enough documentation to assess. [SECP explanation](https://www.secp.gov.pk/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-a-member-and-a-shareholder/). Have a Pakistan corporate lawyer/accountant review a concrete equity/IP/revenue-share proposal before signing; foreign startup examples do not establish its local legal or tax treatment.

## What would a $5 royalty mean?

First define the unit: per card sold, per newly paid profile, per active paid profile/month, or per renewal? State the currency, exchange-rate method if needed, eligibility, discounts/bundles, refunds, taxes, reporting access, payment schedule and duration. Establish whether it continues after leaving and whether support is separately paid. Do not offer indefinite maintenance for an uncertain sales payment.

A percentage of clearly defined collected revenue, or a fixed fee per eligible sale, can be negotiated. Neither is automatically superior. Illustrative arithmetic only: 5% of Rs. 100,000 in eligible collected revenue is Rs. 5,000; if there are no eligible sales, that variable payment is zero. This is not a recommended rate. A fixed US$5 per unit needs a margin calculation against that exact product's sale price and costs. The public subscription prices cannot tell us hardware margin.

Avoid vague net-profit participation without agreed cost definitions, reporting and review rights. Specify exactly which revenue you share; revenue from unrelated web projects, hardware, renewals and software could be different pools. A licence arrangement must also define IP ownership and permitted use. The actual agreement needs local review.

## Protect EdgeTap and define the paid trial

Ask for explicit treatment of existing EdgeTap code, brand, domain, public profiles, customer relationships and independently built future work. Define permitted outside work and overlapping business activities openly. Keep repositories, accounts, credentials, data and project time separate. Any licence of reusable EdgeTap technology would be a separate conscious decision; a pilot should use agreed sample content and the client's properly licensed materials. Do not casually accept a clause assigning every current/future side project to his company.

The trial proposal should record one goal, tasks/out-of-scope work, approved access, hours cap, remote/on-site pattern, fee and payment dates, tools/hosting costs, completion test, revision allowance, handover and termination/payment for work done. Separate ongoing support and emergency response from the delivery promise. A written pilot lets both parties assess collaboration before an ownership commitment.
